# Artistic review — GLM-5.3-Flash #max

Reviewer: GLM-5.3-Flash #max · Scope: composites of the nine recommended picks (`composites/coherence-*.jpg`, `stack-*.png`, `stack-variants.jpg`, `rank-matrix-*.jpg`, `legibility-96-*.jpg`, spot-checked 512 px singles). Read-only except this file. Base documents: `composite-test-plan.md`, `shortlist.md`, `docs/design-guide.md`.

---

## 1. Set coherence — 3/5

The nine **do** share one language: enamel-coin faces, dark fantasy motives, nearly every emblem carries a bright metal rim, one shared dark field behind all of them. That glue is real — this is not nine unrelated medallions. What drags the score to 3 is that the axes the collection claims to have (rank, family) are expressed inconsistently, and the faces themselves drift in field treatment.

Specific sources of "cobbled together":

- **Rank treatment is random across the set (biggest offender).** In stack A, whether a ring shows depends almost entirely on colour accident. The rank-matrix circle sheet proves it: on the journey frame, **bronze is nearly invisible** (blends into the ferry's gold rim) and **gold is invisible** (fuses with gold rim AND gold frame — in the matrix's gold cell the ring disappears completely). Silver and mithril pop crisply. So in the circle coherence grid Weathertop and Buckleberry look like 2-ring badges while Khazad-dûm looks like a 3-ring badge, purely because of rank metal. A collection that is *supposed* to graduate by rank instead looks like it randomly forgot rings on some badges.
- **Background/field drift.** Lothlorien and Breaking sit on clean navy/navy enamel fields; Weathertop sits on granite grey; Mount Doom is a full-bleed red field; Home Again runs a full rainbow gradient field; the two door badges are literal window-scene compositions (door + porch eaves, arch + brick + stars + holly). Four density extremes (Lothlorien symbol-minimal vs Khazad-dûm dense architecture) plus four field extremes (navy, grey, red, rainbow). There is no shared field discipline — no common vignette, no common tonal base — so the grid rows do not rhyme.
- **Habit vs journey split degrades to chrome-vs-gold, which misreads as rank.** At 96 px the habit badges (First Steps, Faithful Walker) have a bright brushed-steel outer band; all journey badges have a gold laurel band. A viewer scanning the set will read "silver badge vs gold badges" — i.e. as if the *habits were a lower/higher tier*. The one differentiator that actually says "habit" — the copper inner line and seven notches on `frame/habit` — dies at 96 px (the copper line is invisible in the legibility sheet). Family identity is being carried by the wrong signal.
- **Frame weight is fine, rank weight is not.** Both family frames are broad and equal-weighted (good — the braided gold/steel journey band and notched chrome habit band sit at the same visual mass). The thin ring between emblem and frame is the odd one out: 80 px wide (less after the emblem's shadow), floats inside a dark well, and reads as a decorative seam, not as a rank object.
- **Which badges break the set hardest:** the two habit badges in A (rank invisible: Faithful Walker's gold ring vanishes against its gold emblem rim; First Steps' bronze is a faint warm seam), and within journey, the two brick-coloured rank cases (Buckleberry, Weathertop) whose rings never read. Home Again at Sunset is the most "foreign" face (rainbow field + wreath saturation) but is rescued by its composition.

## 2. Rank + frame — A / C / D / E compared

- **A (rank inside frame): does not integrate.** The rank is a thin school-tie stripe lost in the gap between two thicker metals. Where it does read (silver/mithril) it reads as a *third decorative ring*, not as rank — the rank-matrix makes modernist nonsense of bronze and gold. Verdict: the identity of "this is a rank grade" is carried by nothing structural; only colour contrast, which the journey family's gold-everything defeats.
- **C (rank inlay on the frame's inner edge): partially integrates.** In the stack-variants sheet the scaled beaded band sits as a bezel against the frame's inner edge — this is the first variant where the rank and frame *touch*, so the set reads as one object. Two failures: (1) for gold rank the bezel + gold emblem rim + gold frame compress into one continuous gold mass — you lose the band boundaries at 96 px; (2) it still reads as "fancier frame ornament", not rank. But as a hierarchy it is honest: one face, one bezel, one frame.
- **D (no rank): the cleanest object.** Face → dark well → family frame. At 96 px it's the best-looking construction in the test. It is also the variant that admits the rank ring adds nothing where it currently sits. If the rank cannot be made to read, D is the correct visual answer and rank belongs in UI metadata (name/progress), not in the art.
- **E (rank as outer border): solves rank-read for 3 of 4 ranks and breaks family.** Evidence: bronze rims on First Steps/Weathertop genuinely read as a bronze border; silver stays crisp; mithril reads — but reads as **chrome/steel**, not elven pale-blue (Home Again at Sunset E's outer twisted steel band with studs could be mistaken for the habit frame's material). Gold rank still fuses with gold frames (Faithful Walker's E is literally two gold rings separated by steel — emblem's own rim inside, rank beads outside — neither identifies as rank at 96 px).
  E's structural break: it eats the frame's identity motif. The scaled ring covers the outer ~92 px of the 136 px band; the journey laurel sits mid-band and **is erased** (Home Again E retains only a thin inner gold sliver; Mount Doom hexagon-E keeps only corner fleurons). The habit frame survives E only because its notch rhythm happens to span the whole band. Result: an asymmetric set where journey badges lose their family signature and habit badges keep theirs. E also *forces* the emblem scale-up to 880, which closes the dark well that gave every badge its depth; combined with the emblem's own rim now touching the remnant frame band, the composition reads compressed.
- **Does the rank ring read as a rank?** In no current variant does a naive viewer parse "bronze/silver/gold/mithril grade" reliably. A / C read "ornament", E reads "rim colour" at best. The rank *stud counts* (3/4/5/6) — the only semantic encoding inside the ring art — vanish at every scale except 512 px close look.
- **What breaks in E specifically:** emblem-to-frame gap closure (depth loss), journey laurel erasure, gold-badge double-gold rims, mithril-as-chrome misread, and hexagon-E's chrome corner caps (see §3).

## 3. Shape — circle

**The masked hexagon test is unfair and it visibly fails, so it cannot carry the decision.** The circular art masked to a hexagon produces the "coin in a tray" error everywhere: Mount Doom (hexagon A, hexagon-E) shows the red face cut by chords with crescent voids between the circular face and the hexagonal band; the baked ember ring is clipped top and bottom; the unwrapped laurel band thins out at the vertices; Home Again's wreath leaves dark corner voids inside the frame. All three outlines — emblem rim, rank band, frame band — disagree about the geometry.

**The bespoke hexagon is the fair test — and it confirms the potential while also confirming the cost.** The two bespoke subjects (First Steps, Buckleberry) are the strongest *object* designs in the whole folder: the portal/door scene genuinely belongs in a hexagon, the emblem edge meets the band naturally. But three things stop me from going hexagon today:

1. **It is a different art style.** The bespoke emblems are painted illustrations with perspective, stone floors, vine overgrowth and real lighting; the production circle set is enamel coinwork. Adopting hexagon means regenerating all nine in the bespoke language plus a re-shortlist — a second full run, with the style match left to luck. The two samples also keep the same flaw the circle set has: in bespoke variant A the **bronze rank is invisible again** (swallowed between the door's wooden surround and the chrome frame). Switching shape does not fix the rank problem.
2. **Geometry tax at small sizes.** Pointy-top hexagon is 880 wide × 1016 tall (manifest) — ~13% narrower content and ~17% less area than a 1016 circle in the same card box. At 96 px, where thumbs are already at the legibility limit, the hexagon scenes read smaller. The 96 px sheet confirms: the hexagon set survives, but no better than the circle set.
3. **Corner economy.** Hexagons need more grid padding or their vertices collide in profile rows/cards; the plan has no badge-shape/grid rule yet — this would also have to be invented for hexagon.

**Recommendation: circle.** The nine existing emblems passed shortlist review in circular form; the circle build is one compositing pass away; the only blocker (rank integration) is a stacking/treatment problem, not a shape problem. Record a shape rule in the design guide: **achievement badges are circular medallions**; keep the two bespoke hexagon masters archived as the seed for a future optional family (e.g. challenge/community) rather than spending a generation run now.

## 4. Stack — OTHER

None of A/C/D/E integrates rank + frame with the current assets, and the reason is structural: in every variant the rank is a thin band adjacent to a gold object (emblem rim in A/C, gold frame in E), so bronze/gold grades can never separate. The fix is to move the rank colour onto a surface where there is no competing gold:

**OTHER — rank as frame plating (rank-tinted frame), no rank ring layer:**

- Keep D geometry: emblem 800 → family frame 880–1016, no ring.
- Composite a **rank-metal plating over the frame band**: multiply/overlay-tint the frame's metal to the rank alloy (aged bronze, polished silver, deep gold, pale blue-white). Family identity = band *shape* (laurel vs notches — survives tinting, as the rank-matrix's mithril cell already proves a pale band over this frame stays legible); rank identity = the colour of the whole ring, read as "plating", not as a third band offset from the frame.
- Add one **rank roundel at 6 o'clock** on the band (a 56–64 px disc in rank metal, replacing/reusing one frame notch position) as the small-size anchor — a single colour point that survives 96 px where full-band tint contrast (bronze on gold) may be marginal.
- Fallback if plating proof fails: **C inlay**, with the emblem's baked rims dimmed on gold-rank badges so the bezel isn't gold-on-gold.

Why not E even as modified: E's outer-rim idea is right in spirit (colour at silhouette = strongest read) but as implemented it costs the journey family its motif and makes chrome read as rank. A slim outer fillet (rank band masked to ~30–40 px) preserves the frame but at 96 px the fillet is ~2 px — below the legibility gate — and the stud art cannot survive that mask cleanly. Plating keeps everything the sets already have and only changes colour placement.

## 5. Fixes, ranked

### Must
1. **Rank colour system** — applies to all nine composites. Replace the ring placement with the OTHER plating treatment (frame-band tint in rank metal + 6 o'clock roundel); verify against the rank matrix at 512 and 96. Fallback: C inlay. Kill criterion: in a 4-rank × hero-badge matrix, all four grades must be distinguishable by a viewer who does not know the alloys.
2. **Emblem rim vs rank collision** — applies to all nine emblem masters (raster step, no regeneration): where a thin rank band remains adjacent to the face, the emblem's baked rim must not compete — mask/dim the emblem rim to ≤40% of its current width, or keep emblems untouched and never place a band there (D-based geometry does this for free).
3. **96 px gate for rank** — applies to the composite-test acceptance criteria: any chosen stack must be re-rendered at 96 px per rank (4 ranks × hero badge minimum) before sign-off; current A and E would fail this for bronze and gold.
4. **If synthesis nevertheless picks E:** cap the ring coverage so the family motif survives — the journey laurel must remain ≥50% of the band (Home Again at Sunset E and Mount Doom hexagon-E currently fail this), emblem back to 800 with the frame's opening retained, and mithril's cool cast verified against the chrome habit band to kill the "chrome = mithril" misread.

### Should
5. **Field discipline pass** — applies to `emblem/…weathertop-survivor`, `journey-frodo-sam-mordor-threshold`, `journey-frodo-sam-home-at-sunset`, `emblem/habit-first-steps` (the grey, red, rainbow and window-scene outliers): add a shared dark interior vignette/blending step inside each emblem's rim so face fields sit in one tonal family. Cheap raster unification; defer any regen until after UI use shows real need.
6. **Strengthen the habit signature** — applies to `frame/habit`: widen/copper the inner line and notch caps so they survive 96 px (currently the copper line disappears, leaving habits to read as "silver vs gold" rank confusion). One frame, one edit, nine-badge benefit.
7. **Bespoke hexagon archive note** — applies to `composites/hexagon-bespoke/`: record in `selections.md` that these are the answer to "what would hexagon art be" and are parked, not lost; the bespoke grid also shows triple-hexagon outlines in its A stack (emblem bevel + rank + frame), so any future hexagon build must fold rank+frame into a single chamfered bezel and widen grid spacing to ≥1.15×.
8. **Representative-rank labelling** — applies to `stack-*.png` and `stack-variants.jpg`: the sheets demo a representative gold rank on a badge whose real grade is bronze (documented in the manifest). Add the real-grade composites alongside so variant rows don't over-represent the already-failing gold case.

### Nice
9. **Emblem footprint normalisation** — check each emblem's alpha bbox fills the 800 box exactly; the dark well width varies slightly badge to badge (Buckleberry vs First Steps), which is part of why the A stack looks hand-fitted rather than systematic.
10. **Stud-count storytelling** — if the plating/roundel treatment is adopted, echo the 3/4/5/6 stud counts in the roundel (or in a UI tooltip) so the rank art's semantic layer is finally usable.

### Recommended next step for the spec
Before 8.6 compositing, add a small **variant F (rank plating) proof** to `composite-test-plan.md`: two frames (journey, habit) × four rank platings, rendered at 512 and 96, judged against the existing A/C/D/E rows in `stack-variants.jpg`. Decision rule: F wins if all four grades read at 96 px and the journey laurel stays identifiable; otherwise C with rim-dimming. No art regeneration in either branch.
