# Artistic review — DeepSeek V4.1 Flash #max

**Scope:** the nine recommended badges as a set, judged on the composites at 512 px and on the 96 px sheets. Read-only review; no compositing or generation.

## 1. Set coherence — 3/5

The set is recognisably one product: same 1016 outer / 880 opening geometry, same top-left key light, same enamel-medallion medium, same gold/steel family frames. It is not yet one intentional collection. Six specific things break it.

| Badge | Family | Rank | Field | Density | Rank read in A |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Steps | habit | bronze | none, edge-to-edge | very high | merges with copper line |
| Buckleberry Ferry | journey | bronze | glossy green/navy dome | medium | merges with gold |
| Weathertop | journey | bronze | matte navy | medium | merges with gold |
| Khazad-dûm | journey | silver | matte grey stone | high | distinct (cool on gold) |
| Golden Wood | journey | silver | glossy navy dome | low | merges with silver emblem rim |
| Rauros | journey | gold | matte navy | low | invisible (gold on gold) |
| Mordor | journey | gold | matte red | medium | invisible (gold on gold) |
| Home at Sunset | journey | mithril | full-bleed pastel gradient | high | distinct (cool on gold) |
| Faithful Walker | habit | gold | glossy cobalt dome | medium | merges with gold/copper |

**1.1 Four field treatments — the biggest tell.**
- Glossy dome + hard white specular: `buckleberry-ferry`, `lothlorien-guest`, `faithful-walker`. These read as app icons.
- Matte flat: `weathertop`, `through-khazad-dum`, `rauros-parting`, `mordor-threshold`. These read as painted plates.
- Full-bleed high-key gradient: `home-at-sunset`. Reads as a storybook postcard.
- No field at all: `first-steps`. The door is edge-to-edge and cropped.

At 96 px the eye sees two lighting models (hard specular vs none), one pastel gradient, and one crop — not one collection. The dome highlight is the "generic mobile game" tell more than the shape is.

**1.2 Palette outliers.** The core is navy/blue + gold; green and grey are motif-justified. Two badges sit outside it:
- `home-at-sunset`: magenta roses, pink-lavender sky, orange gradient. The only pastel/high-key badge; at 96 px it is a pink blob and the weakest read in the set.
- `faithful-walker`: pure cobalt + very bright gold path. The most saturated badge and the most mobile-game read.

`first-steps` is green/brown (motif-justified) but its value range is much brighter than the journey badges, so it sits slightly outside the dark-fantasy key.

**1.3 Motif density ranges from enamel pin to painted plate.** `lothlorien-guest` and `rauros-parting` are ~60% empty field; they read as pins. `first-steps` and `through-khazad-dum` are edge-to-edge; they read as miniature paintings. There is no density rule, so the set has no consistent fill at 96 px: the leaf and horns look small, the door and arch look crowded.

**1.4 Frame weight and language: the habit pair is a different product line.** Both families use the same 880–1016 band (68 px nominal), so the geometry matches. But `frame/journey` is a gold laurel with a beaded outer rim, a dark recessed ground and high-frequency leaf detail — heavy, medieval, minted. `frame/habit` is a smooth brushed-steel band with a thin copper pinstripe and seven regular notches — light, machined, industrial/steampunk. At 96 px the journey frames read as ornate gold rings; the habit frames read as plain chrome washers. The habit pair (`first-steps`, `faithful-walker`) is the only place the set looks like two SKUs.

**1.5 Rank treatment is inconsistent by construction.** In A the rank ring is visually distinct on only two of nine badges (`khazad-dum`, `home-at-sunset`); on the other seven it merges with the emblem's baked rim or the frame's metal. The set shows two rank states while the labels claim four. See §2.

**1.6 Habit vs journey is carried by frame metal, not motif language.** `first-steps` (habit) and `home-at-sunset` (journey) are both round hobbit doors; `faithful-walker` (habit) is a winding path, i.e. a journey image. At 96 px the habit pair reads as journey badges with steel frames. The frame re-cut fixes the weight; the motif language needs a ritual/repetition cue in the next emblem run.

**Which badges break the set:** `home-at-sunset` (palette + field + key), `faithful-walker` (saturation + frame language), `first-steps` (crop + density + frame language), `rauros-parting` and `mordor-threshold` (rank vanishes). `khazad-dum` is the most on-guide; `weathertop` is the best motif/frame balance.

3/5: promising but unfinished. The frame family and the enamel medium hold it together; the field treatment and the habit frame pull it apart.

## 2. Rank + frame — A / C / D / E

**The rank ring is a third decorative ring, not a rank.** At the 1016 master the band is 40 px (A/C) or 46 px (E); at 96 px that is 3.8 / 4.4 px. The studs (3/4/5/6 per tier) are ~2 px at 96 and too small to count. The only surviving signal is metal colour, and four metals collapse to two families at small size: bronze ≈ gold (warm), silver ≈ mithril (cool). The rank matrix confirms it: the four columns are nearly identical at a glance, and at 96 px they would be the same badge with a slightly different rim tint.

**A — rank inside frame (800–880).** Preserves the frame and the emblem, but the rank sits between two metal lines: the emblem's baked rim inside and the frame's inner rim outside. On `first-steps` the bronze rank merges with the habit frame's copper pinstripe; on `buckleberry-ferry` and `weathertop` the bronze merges with the gold laurel; on `rauros-parting` and `mordor-threshold` the gold rank is the same gold as the frame's beaded rim. Only `khazad-dum` (silver on gold) and `home-at-sunset` (mithril on gold) give the ring enough contrast to read as a separate element — and even there it reads as a cool liner, not a rank. A is safe but not a rank system.

**C — rank inlay (920/840).** The only variant that changes the relationship: it moves the rank onto the frame's inner edge and covers the frame's inner rim, so the stack goes from three rings to one bezel. That is a real improvement in integration. But (a) the band is still 40 px, so it is still a liner at 96 px; (b) the beaded texture scales into a pearl/bead bezel, which reads as jewellery, not rank; (c) on gold-on-gold (`rauros`, `mordor`) it collapses into a wider gold bezel with no rank read; (d) it eats the frame's inner rim, which is part of the frame's identity. On a silver-rank badge it would probably be the strongest of the four; on the gold-rank worst case it is a no-op. C is the best fallback if a ring must stay, but it is not a solution.

**D — frame only.** The cleanest composition. The laurel is the hero, the emblem is the hero, and at 96 px the badge reads as a coin with an ornate rim. It is the only variant that looks intentionally composed rather than layered. Its only failure is that it carries no rank art — acceptable if the UI label carries rank (it already does in the legibility sheet).

**E — rank as outer border (1016/923.6, emblem 880).** E puts the rank in the right position (outermost), but fails on five counts:

1. **Gold-on-gold is invisible.** `rauros-parting` and `mordor-threshold` (both gold rank) show a gold beaded outer rim on a gold laurel frame; the rank is indistinguishable from the frame's own beaded rim. In A the gold rank at least appeared as an inner beaded line; in E it disappears into the silhouette, which is worse because the silhouette is the first thing read.
2. **Bronze-on-gold is weak.** `buckleberry-ferry` and `weathertop` get a bronze outer rim close in value to the gold frame; at 96 px it reads as a slightly dirty gold edge.
3. **The family frame is demoted.** The rank covers the frame's outer 46 of the nominal 68 px, leaving ~22 px of laurel (~2 px at 96 px): the laurel becomes a texture, and the family identity (laurel vs notches) is no longer readable. The silhouette is now the rank ring, so the badge reads "rank-coloured coin with a thin gold line", not "journey badge, gold rank".
4. **The 1.1× emblem scale crops edge-composed art.** `first-steps` loses the top of the door arch and the bottom stone step; `lothlorien-guest` cuts the stem at the bottom and crowds the top leaf; `mordor-threshold` cuts the mountain base; `through-khazad-dum` cuts the bottom step; `home-at-sunset` pushes the wreath into the frame. The emblems were composed for an 800 opening; 880 eats their margin.
5. **It breaks the habit pair.** `first-steps` gets a bronze outer rim, `faithful-walker` a gold one, so the two habit badges no longer share a silhouette. The family signal (steel) is now an inner band, and the rank is the outer identity.

**Does E solve it?** No. It moves the rank from a third ring to the outer rim — a stronger position, but the wrong semantic (a rim is a boundary, not a tier) — and it pays for that with the family frame, the emblem composition and the habit pair. E is the worst of the four for set coherence, even though its intent (rank outermost) is right.

**What actually integrates rank with frame:** a single rank device mounted *on* the frame, not a concentric band. See §4.

## 3. Shape — circle

**The masked hexagon is not evidence.** `coherence-hexagon*.jpg`, `rank-matrix-hexagon.jpg` and `stack-hexagon.png` show circular art cropped into a hexagon. The circular cues survive: the dome highlight is still a circular arc; the vignette is still circular; the baked gold rim appears as little arcs at the hexagon vertices (clearest in `stack-hexagon.png`, layer 1); the composition's horizon and radial balance are still circular. The corners are dead space filled with whatever was at the circle's edge. At 96 px the masked hexagons read as circles with corners — `home-at-sunset` is literally a pink circle in a hexagon with magenta corners. The hexagon frame itself warps acceptably (the laurel follows the edges, the miters are readable), so the failure is the emblem, not the frame geometry.

**The bespoke hexagon is the fair test, and it holds up.** `hexagon-bespoke-grid.jpg` shows compositions built for the hexagon: the boat's water fills the bottom point, the sky fills the top corners; the door's step fills the bottom point, the vines fill the corners. The frame miters are crisp, and the silhouette is distinctive. This is a genuinely good hexagon badge. But it is a different style: darker, matte, more illustrated/engraved, with a black inner shadow and a paper/leather texture; the circular set is glossy enamel with a hard specular. To switch to hexagon you would need all nine emblems re-generated with a style lock (or a deliberate full re-style), not a remap. The plan's "another run" is accurate: nine emblems plus cutout plus shortlist, and the frames/rank would need hex masters or a careful remap.

**Why circle wins:**

1. **The art is circular.** The nine emblems are composed as discs: round doors, round domes, circular vignettes, horizons that terminate at the circle, baked circular rims. The hexagon mask fights all of that.
2. **The rank system is annular.** A concentric ring on a circle is a natural offset; on a hexagon it is a parallel polygon, which reads as a second frame or a liner. The rank device geometry is simpler and stronger on a circle.
3. **On-guide read.** Circle = coin, seal, medallion, heraldic badge. Pointy-top hexagon = mobile-game tile/achievement. The design guide explicitly says no generic mobile-game look. At 96 px the hexagons read as game tiles; the circles read as coins.
4. **Production cost.** The nine circular emblems exist and are shortlisted. Hexagon needs nine bespoke emblems with a style lock, plus re-cutout/re-shortlist, plus hex frame/rank geometry. The bespoke test proves the shape can work, but it does not prove the existing set can move for free.
5. **Grid.** Circles tile cleanly in a 3×3 grid with even margins. Pointy-top hexagons (880 wide × 1016 tall) leave side gaps and read smaller in square cells; the hexagon grids show more dead space around each badge.

**If hexagon is chosen anyway, the must-change list:** (a) nine bespoke hexagon emblems, no masks, with the motif filling the hexagon width and the field filling the corners, no circular vignette/dome/baked rim; (b) style-lock to the existing enamel look or deliberately re-style the whole set (do not mix); (c) remove the frame's inner rim so it does not read as a second frame; (d) no parallel-polygon rank ring — use a vertex-mounted boss or drop rank art; (e) bespoke frame with mitered corner pieces, because the offset polygon stretches the laurel at the vertices; (f) honeycomb/optical grid spacing sized to the hexagon width; (g) 96 px test with a 10–12% motif margin so the vertices do not clip.

## 4. Stack — OTHER (D + frame-mounted rank boss)

**Pick OTHER: frame only (D) plus a single rank boss mounted on the frame at 6 o'clock.**

Concretely:

- **Base:** emblem 800 → frame 880–1016, no 800–880 rank ring. This is D. The laurel/notches are fully visible, the emblem is uncropped, and the stack has two rings (emblem rim + frame), not four.
- **Rank device:** a 64–72 px (on the 1016 master) cabochon/boss set into the frame band at 6 o'clock, replacing/augmenting the frame's bottom clasp. Rank metal for the cabochon (bronze/silver/gold/mithril), dark iron bezel so it separates from both gold and steel. The boss is a single mark, not a ring, so it adds no concentric element.
- **Tier encoding:** the cabochon cut carries a second axis (round = 1, square = 2, hex = 3, star = 4), because metal colour alone collapses to two families at 96 px. At 96 px the cut is not countable, so the UI label carries the exact tier; the art carries the metal family and the collectible "set gem" cue.
- **Why this is better:** it integrates rank with the frame (the rank is *on* the family frame, not floating between emblem and frame); it preserves the frame's silhouette and motif; it does not crop the emblem; it works on gold and steel because of the dark bezel; it is one small asset per tier (4), no emblem regen, no frame regen; it is legible at 96 px as a coloured dot at 6 o'clock, with the UI label naming the tier.
- **Fallback if a ring must stay:** C, not A or E. C at least changes the relationship and gives a two-tone bezel on silver/mithril ranks. But C still fails gold-on-gold and still eats the frame's inner rim; it is a patch, not a system.

## 5. Fixes

### MUST

1. **Drop the concentric rank ring; composite D + frame boss.** Applies to `rank/*` (a-*), `frame/journey` (a-271828), `frame/habit` (a-112358), all nine composites. The ring is 3.8–4.4 px at 96 px and reads as a third rim; gold-on-gold vanishes on `rauros`/`mordor`; bronze merges with the habit frame's copper line on `first-steps`. This is the single biggest cause of the cobbled read.
2. **Unify the field treatment.** Applies to all nine `emblem/*`. One rule: matte flat base + soft top-light gradient, no hard white dome. Remove the dome from `buckleberry-ferry`, `lothlorien-guest`, `faithful-walker`; add the same soft gradient to `weathertop`, `khazad-dum`, `rauros`, `mordor`; flatten `home-at-sunset`. A grade/LUT pass with a masked specular knock-back is enough; no regen if the dome is a separate layer.
3. **Fix `home-at-sunset`: swap to `b-161803`.** Applies to `emblem/journey-frodo-sam-home-at-sunset`. `b-161803` is aged bronze with an amber/rust sunset and muted roses; it fixes the palette and the 96 px read without a grade. If the brighter `a-112358` is a brand requirement, grade it down: sunset to amber/rust/burgundy, roses to deep crimson, key −15%, keep the lit windows as the only high-value accent.
4. **Fix `first-steps`: swap to `b-141421`.** Applies to `emblem/habit-first-steps`. `a-112358` is cropped at the arch and stone step by its own source circle; `b-141421` has the full door, step and margin, and is more aged/on-guide. No composite scale can recover the cut arch.
5. **Re-cut `frame/habit` to the journey frame's weight and world.** Applies to `frame/habit` (a-112358). Gunmetal instead of chrome; copper beaded outer rim to match the journey frame's beaded edge; hammered band; hand-cut/irregular notches; keep the copper inner line. One frame asset, cheap; fixes the "chrome washer" read at 96 px.
6. **Remove the emblems' baked rims from the visible stack.** Applies to the nine `emblem/*` with a baked gold/silver rim. Mask the rim out of the emblem layer and feather the outer 8–12 px into the field, rather than scaling the art up; scaling to 880 is exactly what crops E. If the rim is structural, extend the field colour under it so the frame's inner edge is the only rim. The baked rim + rank + frame inner rim is the "too many rings" problem; with the rank ring gone, the baked rim is the remaining offender.

### SHOULD

7. **Normalize motif density.** Applies to all nine `emblem/*`: 55–70% motif coverage, 12–15% field margin. Tighten `lothlorien-guest`/`rauros` slightly, widen `first-steps`/`khazad-dum`. Re-crop/scale where possible, not regen.
8. **Desaturate `faithful-walker`.** Applies to `emblem/habit-faithful-walker` (a-173205): cobalt −15% saturation, gold path highlight −10% value, add a subtle field gradient. It is the most saturated badge and the most mobile-game read.
9. **Give rank a second axis.** Applies to the new `rank/boss-*` assets: cut encodes tier (round/square/hex/star) plus metal; UI label carries the name. Colour alone cannot carry four tiers at 96 px.
10. **Fix the legibility test coverage.** Applies to `legibility-96-*.jpg`: add A and D at 96 px for the worst case (`buckleberry-ferry`, gold rank) and a silver-rank badge (`khazad-dum`); label the stack-variants' gold rank as "worst case" (the coherence grid shows Ferry as bronze, which is confusing). The current sheet only tests E, so the comparison is not fair.
11. **Normalize frames before the next composite run.** Applies to `frame/journey`, `frame/habit`. The processed frames measure ~993 outer / 872 inner on the alpha bbox, not the plan's 1016/880; the emblem art is ~776, not 800; the rank is ~884/796. The composites are directionally valid, but the frame band is ~61 px instead of 68 px and the rank is relatively wider, which flatters the rank and lightens the frame. Normalize to the plan's geometry before comparing A/C/E at 96 px.
12. **If hexagon is retained, commit to the full bespoke run.** Applies to all nine emblems + hex frames/rank: the must-change list in §3. The masked hexagon is not a valid basis for a decision.

### NICE

13. **Family field texture.** Journey = faint aged parchment/leather grain; habit = faint woven linen/stone. Ties the set without changing motifs.
14. **Family mark at 12 o'clock.** `frame/journey` = small leaf/rune; `frame/habit` = matching iron/copper mark. Keeps the family readable at 96 px when the laurel/notches blur.
15. **1 px dark keyline between emblem and frame.** Composite template. Separates art from metal; makes the emblem sit in the frame.
16. **Test 200 px thumb.** The production thumb is 200 px (shortlist 8.7); the boss and laurel need testing there too.

**Recommended next step for the spec:** add a Badge Art section to `docs/design-guide.md` (or a new `docs/badge-art-spec.md`) fixing: shape = circle; stack = emblem 800 → frame 880–1016, no concentric rank ring; rank = 6 o'clock frame boss in rank metal with a dark bezel + UI rank label (UI is authoritative for the exact tier); field = matte flat + soft top-light, no hard dome; family frames = journey gold laurel / habit forged steel+copper at equal 68 px visual weight; emblem = no baked rim, 55–70% motif coverage. Then re-composite the nine as D+boss and run 96 px and 200 px checks. That is one spec change, four small boss assets, one re-composite, and two emblem swaps (`first-steps` b-141421, `home-at-sunset` b-161803) that are needed anyway.
