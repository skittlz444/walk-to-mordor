## Example Descriptions

### ✅ GOOD Description Examples

#### Goal 1: "Bag End" (distance = 0)

> The story begins not in a king's court or a fortress of old, but in the peaceful, sun-dappled hills of the Shire, within the very heart of the hobbit-hole known as Bag End. Here, amidst the quiet comfort of home, the fate of Middle-earth was sealed. It was upon the occasion of a long-expected party for Bilbo Baggins that a seemingly simple act transpired: the passing of a golden ring to his young heir, Frodo. Yet even in this moment of warmth and celebration, a strange shadow fell, for Bilbo's words turned evasive and his spirit weary, hinting at the sinister power of the heirloom he now bequeathed. This tranquil setting, a sanctuary of innocence, would soon be left behind, its simple comforts serving as a precious memory for the long and perilous road ahead.

**Why it's good:**
- Sets the scene with vivid imagery ("sun-dappled hills", "hobbit-hole")
- References specific events (Bilbo's party, passing of the Ring)
- Captures the emotional tone (warmth mixed with foreboding)
- Unique to this exact moment in the narrative

---

#### Goal 2: "Cross The Water" (distance = 3)

> The initial leg of Frodo's quest began not with a grand march, but as a hurried and clandestine flight from the Shire to evade the relentless pursuit of the Black Riders. Frodo, Sam, and Pippin, their hearts filled with a mix of fear and resolve, traveled by night, leaving the warmth of their hobbit-holes behind. Their path wound through the familiar landscapes of their home, which now seemed alien and menacing under the threat of pursuit. They crossed a small plank bridge over the stream known as "The Water," a mundane act that became their first true step out of the Shire's protective embrace and into a world of ever-present danger.

**Why it's good:**
- Names specific characters (Frodo, Sam, Pippin)
- References the specific location ("The Water")
- Captures the contrast between familiar and threatening
- Explains the narrative significance of this crossing

---

#### Goal 3: "Cross The Great Road" (distance = 5)

> Continuing their frantic journey, the hobbits crossed the Great Road, a wide path leading from the Brandywine Bridge. This crossing marked their entry into Tookland, a part of the Shire known for its adventurous inhabitants. The hobbits, however, were not in the mood for adventure. Every rustle in the hedgerows and every shadow that lengthened in the pale moonlight was a cause for alarm, for they knew the Black Riders were searching for them. This conscious choice to leave the main road was a desperate strategy to outwit their pursuers, moving them ever deeper into the uncertain wilderness of Middle-earth.

**Why it's good:**
- References specific places (Great Road, Brandywine Bridge, Tookland)
- Adds character detail ("known for its adventurous inhabitants")
- Vivid sensory details ("rustle in the hedgerows", "pale moonlight")
- Explains WHY they made this choice

### ❌ BAD Description Examples

#### Goal: "Cross a small stream" (distance = 627)

> The Fellowship crossed a small stream, continuing their progress through the wild lands toward the Misty Mountains. These small, unrecorded events of travel were a testament to the sheer scale of the epic quest. They were constantly navigating the natural landscape, following the lay of the land to reach their final destination, with the looming threat of Saruman's spies and the watchful Eye of Sauron following them.

**Why it's bad:**
- Completely generic - could apply to dozens of stream crossings
- Uses filler phrases ("testament to the sheer scale")
- No specific details about THIS particular stream or moment
- Repetitive structure seen in many other descriptions

---

#### Goal: "Camp by a small stream" (distance = 2509)

> The hobbits camped by a small stream as they continued their journey north through the Gap of Rohan. This was a small, mundane detail in the long and arduous journey, but it added to the sense of realism and the difficult terrain they had to traverse. The constant movement, with little rest, was a testament to the immense peril they had endured.

**Why it's bad:**
- Admits it's "mundane" - never call attention to how generic something is
- Uses identical phrasing found in many other camp descriptions
- "Testament to the immense peril" is overused throughout
- No unique details about this specific camp location or moment

---

#### Goal: "Camp near marshes" (distance = 3391)

> The hobbits camped for the night, with the marshes lying farther north from the road. They were nearing their final destination of Bree. The camp was a temporary refuge, a moment of brief, anxious slumber before they must continue their long journey. The journey was a long and arduous one, and the constant need to stop and rest highlighted the slow, difficult nature of their quest.

**Why it's bad:**
- Self-referential ("The journey was a long and arduous one" repeats the same idea twice)
- Reuses the exact phrase "a moment of brief, anxious slumber" found in other descriptions
- Generic "temporary refuge" language used elsewhere
- Doesn't capture anything unique about returning home after saving the world

## Migration File Naming Convention

New migration files for description updates should follow:
```
migrations/00XX_update_goal_descriptions_YY.sql
```

Where:
- `XX` = Next available migration number (currently would be 0012)
- `YY` = Batch number (01, 02, 03, etc.)

Each file should contain **20 UPDATE statements** (except possibly the last file).
