## What Went Well ✅

1. **Map UX quality landed strongly**
   - Project lead feedback: the map looks great and the tiling implementation worked very well.
   - The map experience shipped with high visual fidelity and practical usability.

2. **Technical architecture held under complexity**
   - The team successfully integrated map shell, path rendering, marker behavior, popup overlays, and map state orchestration.
   - Preference bridging between legacy JS and Preact islands proved viable for cross-cutting features.

3. **Foundational map capabilities are production-enabling**
   - Position/path/waypoint logic and preference-driven rendering create a strong base for social and collaborative features in Epic 3.

---

## What Didn’t Go Well ❌

1. **Playwright flakiness, especially in CI**
   - Test instability reduced confidence in pass/fail as a quality signal.
   - Flakiness contributed to story-level delivery disruption.

2. **Story cancellation tied to CI reliability failure**
   - Story 2-9 was canceled because CI behavior was not dependable enough to support the intended delivery.

3. **Requirements drift**
   - Some requirements were not followed.
   - Requirement changes were also not consistently propagated or honored downstream.

---

## Root Cause Themes (System, Not People)

1. Missing deterministic CI contract for visual/E2E map tests.
2. Lack of mandatory requirement-change handshake before continued implementation.
3. Insufficient downstream propagation process for requirement deltas into future stories.

## Action Items for Epic 3 🎯

### A1. CI Reliability Gate (Mandatory)
**Owner:** Dana (QA) + Charlie (Senior Dev)  
**Category:** Quality / Process  
**Success Criteria:**
- Critical Playwright suite demonstrates stable repeatability in CI.
- Deterministic controls are enforced for visual/E2E tests (clock/data/viewport/animation stabilization where applicable).
- Any quarantined test has explicit owner, reason, and exit criteria.

### A2. Requirement-Change Control Gate (Mandatory)
**Owner:** Alice (PO) + Charlie (Senior Dev) + Bob (Scrum Master)  
**Category:** Product / Process  
**Success Criteria:**
- Requirement delta is recorded in the active story artifact.
- Revised ACs are acknowledged by Product and Dev before coding resumes.
- Test plan and AC traceability are updated for the active story.

### A3. Future Story Propagation Rule (New, Non-Negotiable)
**Owner:** Bob (Scrum Master)  
**Category:** Governance / Planning  
**Success Criteria:**
- Any accepted requirement change triggers a scan of impacted future stories.
- Impacted future stories are updated for ACs, dependencies, sequencing, and risk notes.
- Stories with unresolved change impact are explicitly flagged.
- **No new impacted story may start while unresolved `change-impact` flags remain.**

### A4. DoD Traceability Check
**Owner:** Charlie (Senior Dev) + Dana (QA)  
**Category:** Quality  
**Success Criteria:**
- No story is marked done if accepted requirements lack either:
  - automated test coverage, or
  - explicit manual verification notes.
