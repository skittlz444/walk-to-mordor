## What Went Well

1. **Implementation quality was unusually high for the breadth of the epic**
   - Hayden described the work as essentially one-shot with little correction needed.
   - The story artifacts support that assessment: follow-up changes were mostly review tightening, extensions, edge-case fixes, and integration polish rather than structural rescue work.

2. **The sequencing was strong and reduced rework**
   - Story 6.1 established schema, friend codes, avatar assets, and validation first.
   - Stories 6.2 and 6.3 built the social API and fellowship-invite flows on top of that stable data model.
   - Stories 6.4 through 6.6 then layered in the Friends UI, reusable Avatar component, and map-social integration.

3. **Epic 6 created a coherent social layer rather than disconnected features**
   - Friend relationships, avatars, fellowship invites, profile identity, and map visibility all fit together as one product surface.
   - The result is strategically valuable because it unlocks stronger identity and discovery patterns for future race work.

4. **Review loops improved the product without derailing delivery**
   - The story records repeatedly mention meaningful code-review fixes.
   - Those fixes strengthened correctness and integration quality without forcing major restarts.

5. **The epic delivered visible user-facing value**
   - The app now feels socially coherent rather than merely having isolated multi-user capabilities.
   - Friends, avatars, and friends-on-map materially changed the product feel.

## What Did Not Go Well

1. **Testing was the dominant weakness again**
   - Hayden identified testing as the main struggle from the epic.
   - Coverage remained incomplete in too many places, especially around frontend/UI verification.
   - Flakiness was introduced, reducing confidence in the suite.

2. **The verification path was weaker than the implementation path**
   - The code itself largely landed in one shot.
   - The environment and setup for reliable verification were still awkward enough that closure drifted.
   - This created a mismatch: implementation felt straightforward while proving correctness felt expensive.

3. **Frontend completion discipline was still too easy to defer**
   - Story 6.4 explicitly skipped Playwright follow-up.
   - Story 6.5 deferred some verification work.
   - Story 6.6 deferred Playwright and some supporting follow-up work.
   - The bookkeeping was honest, but the closeout bar was still too soft.

4. **Some fine-detail UI ambiguities still surfaced late**
   - These issues were described by Hayden as small UI items rather than major delivery problems.
   - They were cheap to fix and did not indicate systemic planning or implementation instability.

## Root Cause Themes

1. **Verification infrastructure is the primary bottleneck**
   - The dominant issue was not coding correctness.
   - The dominant issue was the lack of a repeatable, low-friction test setup that behaves well both locally and in CI.

2. **Awkward setup encourages testing deferral**
   - When frontend and integration verification are costly to start and maintain, teams rationalize leaving them for later.
   - In Epic 6 that pattern appeared again in deferred or incomplete UI verification.

3. **Flakiness compounds process damage**
   - Once flaky tests enter the suite, failures become easier to discount.
   - That weakens trust in the quality signal and makes definition-of-done enforcement less meaningful.

4. **Small UI ambiguities were refinement issues, not planning collapse**
   - The remaining non-testing issues were mostly fine-detail ambiguities.
   - They were fixed cheaply and should not be mistaken for major execution failure.

## Deep Story Analysis

### Execution Patterns

- Story 6.1 established the schema and identity substrate: friendships, avatar support, friend codes, and static avatar assets.
- Story 6.2 created the main friend API surface and enforced route ownership, auth correctness, and duplicate-prevention rules.
- Story 6.3 extended the social model into fellowship invitations while reusing existing party-join semantics instead of forking behavior.
- Story 6.4 exposed the social backend through Friends pages, profile surfaces, and fellowship invite UI.
- Story 6.5 consolidated identity rendering through a reusable Avatar component and profile-based avatar selection.
- Story 6.6 completed the epic by bringing friend visibility to the map through a unified Social panel and friend markers.

### Quality Patterns

- Backend rigor remained consistently strong across the epic.
- Review findings were real and materially improved the implementation.
- Route ordering and `getAllowedMethods()` remained recurring safety concerns, but the team consistently caught and handled them.
- Frontend automation and final verification remained less consistent than backend verification.

### Common Struggles

- Playwright and UI verification were repeatedly deferred or softened.
- Test setup friction likely contributed directly to incomplete closure and flakiness.
- Documentation alignment improved in some stories but was still deferred in others.

### Breakthroughs

- The social identity model was established once and reused cleanly across the stack.
- The reusable Avatar component removed inline duplication and made identity rendering consistent across features.
- The Social panel on the map unified fellowship view and friend visibility into a coherent user-facing control surface.

## Closing Notes

Epic 6 was successful. The implementation landed with unusually little correction for a feature set of this breadth, and the result materially improved the product by making its social layer coherent and extensible.

The dominant lesson from the epic is not about coding quality. It is about verification infrastructure. The team is capable of shipping the code. The system still makes it too hard to prove that the code is reliably finished.

The other issues from Epic 6 were mostly fine-detail UI ambiguities and polish-level adjustments. Those were easy to fix and should not be confused with major execution instability.

The strategic result of this retrospective is clear:

1. Epic 6 is complete, live, accepted, and stable.
2. Epic 5 remains the right next feature area.
3. Epic 5 must be fully reviewed and re-planned against current reality before execution begins.
4. A repeatable local-and-CI test setup is now a top readiness priority for the next UI-heavy epic.
